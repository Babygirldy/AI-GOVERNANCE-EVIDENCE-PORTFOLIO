# Day 03 — Healthcare AI Governance: SepsisWatch AI

**Date drafted:** 7 November 2026  
**Case:** Fictional Safe Lives Hospital (England) and fictional supplier MedSignal Analytics Ltd.  
**Analyst role simulated:** AI Governance and Technology Risk Analyst.  
**Status: ASSISTANT-PREPARED WORKING CASE (v0.1). NOT AN INDEPENDENT LEARNER-SIGNED ASSESSMENT.**  
**No real patient data, device, hospital approval or supplier testing is involved.**

## Decision question
Should a hospital introduce a machine-learning tool that flags possible sepsis or clinical deterioration using adult inpatient observations and laboratory values?

**Provisional governance recommendation:** **HOLD live clinical deployment** pending documented safety, clinical effectiveness, legal/data protection, procurement and human-oversight evidence. A simulation-only evaluation using synthetic or appropriately governed data could be considered separately, subject to its own approval.

## Intended use (working assumption)
- Alerts trained ward clinicians about possible patient deterioration requiring review.
- Uses recent electronic observations and laboratory results for adult general-ward inpatients.
- Does **not** autonomously diagnose sepsis, change medicines or initiate treatment.
- The clinical team keeps responsibility for diagnosis and care.
- The supplier's actual intended-purpose statement, claims, medical device classification and regulatory status are **not provided** in this fictional scenario.

## Portfolio deliverables
1. [AI system inventory](ai-system-inventory.md): system boundaries, data, owners and key unknowns.
2. [Illustrative AI and clinical risk register](risk-register.md): eight risks, hypothetical inherent scores, controls and required assurance.
3. [AI impact assessment and provisional board recommendation](impact-assessment.md): rights, safety, fairness, legal screening and decision conditions.
4. [NIST AI RMF crosswalk and evidence request register](evidence-plan.md): what proof a reviewer would request.
5. [Official source log](sources.md): relevant regulators, standards and references.

## What has *not* happened
- No clinical validation, supplier due diligence or privacy assessment has been performed on a real product.
- Risk scores are **illustrative**, not measured patient harm probabilities.
- No legal conclusion about whether this specific imaginary product qualifies as a regulated medical device can be made without its full intended-purpose statement.
- No final DPIA, DCB0160 assurance or decision-authority sign-off has been completed.
- No independent learner commentary or oral defence has yet been recorded.

## Learner's four-step walkthrough
1. Start in the inventory. Understand the difference between **known scenario facts**, **assumptions** and **unknown evidence**.
2. Read the eight risk statements. For each, say out loud who could be harmed and how.
3. Read the impact assessment. Identify which board approval conditions address which risks.
4. Choose **one risk** and create an original 150-word critique: explain whether its score seems reasonable and what real evidence could change your judgement. Save with the actual date.

## Why this matters for employability
This mirrors an entry-level governance analyst's workflow: define use case → map stakeholders and legal duties → evaluate hazards and gaps → request evidence → advise decision makers. It is a **training portfolio**, not a substitute for clinical or legal professional sign-off.
